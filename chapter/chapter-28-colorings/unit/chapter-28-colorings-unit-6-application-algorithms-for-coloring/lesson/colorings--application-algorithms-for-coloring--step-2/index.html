Approximate Algorithms. The algorithms listed above are, of course, not practical. One might ask: if a graph is 3-colorable, how can we quickly find a coloring using not too many colors?

Theorem (Wigderson, 1983). Any \(3\)-colorable graph on \(n\) vertices can be colored in \(3 \sqrt n\) colors in polynomial time.

Proof. We proceed as follows: while the graph has a vertex of degree at least \(\sqrt n\), color its neighbors with two new colors and remove them from the graph. The neighbors are 2-colorable precisely because we know the original graph is 3-colorable. After no more than \(\sqrt n\) iterations, we are left with a graph of degree less than \(\sqrt n\), which can be colored using \(\sqrt n\) colors.◼
