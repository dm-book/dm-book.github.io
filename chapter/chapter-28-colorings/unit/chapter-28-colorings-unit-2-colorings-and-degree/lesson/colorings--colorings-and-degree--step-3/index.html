Colorings · Colorings and Degree

Lesson 3

Nikolai Chukhin · Alexander S. Kulikov

For connected graphs, the inequality \(\chi(G) \le \Delta(G) + 1\) is almost never tight: the only exceptions are complete graphs and odd cycles.

Theorem (Brooks, 1941). If a connected graph \(G\) is neither an odd cycle nor a complete graph, then \(\chi(G) \le \Delta(G)\).

This theorem is not hard to prove, but we will not provide the proof here. We will only show that the main case in the proof of this theorem is regular graphs.

Lemma. If in a connected graph \(G(V,E)\), for which \(\Delta(G) \le d\), there is a vertex of degree less than \(d\), then \(\chi(G) \le d\).

Proof. Induction on the number of vertices \(n\). The base case \(n \le d\) is easy to check. To prove the step \(n-1 \to n\), take a vertex \(v \in V\) of degree less than \(d\) and consider the graph \(G-v\). Let \(G_{1},\dotsc,G_{k}\) be the connected components of this graph. Since \(G\) is connected, each of these graphs has a vertex of degree less than \(d\), so each of them can be colored in \(d\) colors by the inductive hypothesis. Then we just need to color vertex \(v\): one of the \(d\) colors will be available for it because its degree is less than \(d\).◼
