Lesson 3

Nikolai Chukhin · Alexander S. Kulikov

Theorem (Mycielski, 1955). For any \(k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 2}\), there exists a graph \(G_{k}\) such that \(\omega(G_{k})=2\) and \(\chi(G_{k})=k\).

Proof. We will construct the graph \(G_{k}\) with the desired properties sequentially for all \(k \ge 2\). For \(k=2\) it is simply an edge. The graph \(G_{k+1}\) is obtained from the graph \(G_{k}\) as follows. Let \(G_{k}=(V,E)\), where \(V=\{v_{1}, \dotsc, v_{n}\}\). The graph \(G_{k+1}=(V \cup U \cup \{z\},E')\) is obtained from \(G_{k}\) by adding a “mirror copy” of each of its vertices (\(U=\{u_{1}, \dotsc, u_{n}\}\)) and a new vertex \(z\) connected to the entire set \(U\): \[E'=E \cup \{\{z, u_{i}\} \colon i \in [n]\} \cup \{\{u_{i},v_{j}\} \colon i \neq j \in [n],\ \{v_{i},v_{j}\} \in E\} \ .\] The graphs \(G_{2}\), \(G_{3}\), \(G_{4}\) are shown in the previous problem.

It is easy to see that the resulting graph cannot contain triangles. Coloring the graph \(G_{k+1}\) in \(k+1\) colors is also straightforward: color the vertices from \(U\) the same as the corresponding vertices from \(V\), and color the vertex \(z\) with a new color. It remains to show that \(G_{k+1}\) cannot be colored in \(k\) colors. Suppose we managed to color \(G_{k+1}\) with \(k\) colors and suppose vertex \(z\) is colored with color \(k\). Then the vertices in set \(U\) are colored with colors \(\{1, \dotsc, k-1\}\). In the subgraph induced by \(V\), recolor each vertex \(v_{i}\) that has color \(k\) with the color of vertex \(u_{i}\). The resulting coloring of \(V\) remains proper. Indeed, two adjacent vertices \(v_{i}\) and \(v_{j}\) could not both have had color \(k\). If only \(v_{i}\) is recolored, then \(u_{i}\) is adjacent to \(v_{j}\) by construction, so the new color of \(v_{i}\) differs from the color of \(v_{j}\). Thus, we obtain a proper coloring of \(G_{k}\) with \(k-1\) colors—a contradiction with \(\chi(G_{k})=k\).◼
