Nikolai Chukhin · Alexander S. Kulikov

Theorem (Mantel, 1907). Let \(G(V,E)\) be a graph with \(n\) vertices and \(\omega(G)=2\). Then \[|E| \le \frac{n^2}{4}\ .\]

Proof. Induction on \(n\). Base cases \(n=1,2\) are checked directly. Step \(n-2 \to n\): take two adjacent vertices \(u\) and \(v\). It is easy to see that \(\deg(u)+\deg(v) \le n\). Let \(H=G-\{u,v\}\). Then \[\begin{align*}|E(G)|&\leq \frac{(n-2)^2}{4}+ (n-1)=\\&= \frac{(n^2-4n+4)}{4}+ (n-1)=\\&= \frac{n^2}{4}-(n-1) + (n-1)=\\&= \frac{n^2}{4}\ .\end{align*}\]◼

For the curious 🤓

Theorem (Turán, 1941). Let \(G(V,E)\) be a graph with \(n\) vertices and \(\omega(G)=r\). Then \[|E| \le \left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\cdot \frac{n^2}{2}\ .\]We omit the proof. It follows the same extremal philosophy as Mantel's theorem, but the bookkeeping for the \(r\) parts is more involved.
