Proofs of Algorithm Correctness and Runtime Estimates · Theory Problems

Lesson 1

Nikolai Chukhin · Alexander S. Kulikov

Basic Problems.

  1. (5 points) You are given \(n\) stones with different weights. In one turn, you can compare two stones. How many comparisons are needed to find the heaviest stone?
  2. (10 points) Prove that, for any \(k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}\), \[1^{k} + 2^{k} + \dotsb + n^{k} = \Theta(n^{k+1}).\]
    Hint:
    For the lower bound, consider the last \(n/2\) terms of the sum.
  3. (10 points) Given \(n\) stones with distinct weights. In one turn, you can compare two stones. How many comparisons are needed to identify the heaviest stone non-adaptively? That is, you must specify the list of comparisons in advance, and after performing all of them, you must be able to determine the heaviest stone.
    Hint:
    Consider what information is absolutely necessary to guarantee finding the heaviest stone without making any more questions.
  4. (15 points) On a deserted island, there is a colony of chameleons. Initially, there are \(13\) red, \(15\) green, and \(17\) blue chameleons. They have a peculiar social behavior: whenever two chameleons of different colors meet, they both change their color to the third color. For example, if a green chameleon meets a blue one, they both become red. Is it possible for all the chameleons in the colony to eventually become the same color?
    Hint:
    Consider the difference between the number of red and green chameleons, for example. How does it change after a meeting?
  5. (15 points) Prove that to sort \(n\) objects, one needs \(\Theta(n\log n)\) pairwise comparisons.
    Hint:
    To prove the lower bound, observe that each comparison yields two possible outcomes, thereby constructing a decision tree. What can be said about this tree?
  6. (15 points) Suppose there is a card known to have a non-negative integer \(n\) written on one side and the integer \(n + 1\) on the other side. Alice and Bob are sitting opposite each other, looking at this card from different sides, and the following conversation occurs between them.
    • Alice: I do not know the number on Bob's side.
    • Bob: I do not know the number on Alice's side.
    This is repeated 10 times in total, after which Alice says that she knows the number on Bob's side. What numbers could have been written on the card?
    Hint:
    Let \(A\) be the number Alice sees and \(B\) be the number Bob sees. Since the numbers are \(n\) and \(n+1\), if Alice sees \(A\), then Bob sees either \(A-1\) or \(A+1\). Think what Alice's statement "I do not know the number on Bob's side" implies about the value of \(A\).
  7. (15 points) On the reverse sides of \(2023\) cards, distinct numbers are written (one number on each card). With one query, it is allowed to point to any three cards and learn the set of numbers written on them. What is the minimum number of queries required to determine which numbers are written on each card?
    Hint:
    Consider what happens if a card is never part of a query, or if it's only part of one query.
  8. (15 points) The test consists of 30 questions, each of which has two answer options (one correct and the other incorrect). In one attempt, Alice answers all the questions, after which she is told how many questions she answered correctly. Can Alice act in such a way that she is guaranteed to find all the correct answers no later than after the 24th attempt (and answer all the questions correctly on the 25th attempt)? (Initially, Alice does not know any of the answers, and the test remains the same every time.)
