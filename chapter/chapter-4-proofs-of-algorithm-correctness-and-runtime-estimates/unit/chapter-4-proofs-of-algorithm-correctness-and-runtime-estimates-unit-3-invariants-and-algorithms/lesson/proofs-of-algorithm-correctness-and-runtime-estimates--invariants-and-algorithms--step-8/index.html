We will show that for any population, this process will eventually end. To do this, we will construct a so-called semi-invariant—a numerical parameter that is always non-negative and decreases each time someone in the population starts supporting the opposite team. Semi-invariants are often used to estimate the runtime of algorithms.

Let's call a friendly connection between two residents unstable if these residents support different teams. Below, on the same example, unstable connections are shown with dotted lines.

As you can see, the number of unstable connections decreases each day: initially (on day 0) it is six, then it decreases to four, and then it decreases to two. It turns out this is not a coincidence: for any population, the number of unstable connections decreases each time a resident starts supporting the other team.

To prove this, consider a resident who is about to start supporting the opposite team. We definitely know the reason why she is about to do this: the number of her friends supporting the opposite team is greater than the number of her friends who support her favorite team. In other words, she had more unstable connections before the change than she will have after the change. This is shown in the figure below.

It remains to note that the number of unstable connections is a non-negative integer. This means that it cannot decrease indefinitely, which, in turn, allows us to conclude that our process cannot continue forever.
